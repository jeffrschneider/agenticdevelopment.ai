agentic-development // the ordinary stages, and what changes when an agent holds one

A lifecycle for software that agents build

The stages have not changed. Somebody still has to decide what is wanted, write it, test it, review it, ship it, prove it works, and take it away again. What changes when an agent holds a stage is that the handoff has to become a thing, not a conversation. This is a map of the twenty stages, the role each one needs, and the artifact it hands to the next.

Read the stages The handoffs

Three things, for every stage

A lifecycle that only names its stages is a diagram. What makes one usable is saying, for each stage, who is meant to hold it, what they are handed, and how anyone can tell it is finished. Those three questions are the whole of this map.

01

The role

The job, written before any particular agent exists to hold it. Roles are published separately, at agentroles.ai, so a stage here names a role rather than a product. Several stages have no published role yet, and that is worth saying out loud.

02

The handoff

What the stage is given and what it hands back, each an artifact somebody can name, read and check. A stage whose output is a conversation cannot be held by an agent, however capable the agent is.

03

What done means

The check that says the stage really happened, made against something written down beforehand. Without it, a stage held by an agent is a stage nobody is accountable for.

What the map shows

The middle, from Build to Acceptance, is where agents already hold the work. That stretch was built out of artifacts before anybody thought about agents. Either end is held by people.

The front end is stuck on Code, Test, Review and Security review, where there is no artifact to pass. The back end is stuck on Eval, Publish, Cost, Incident response and Rollback, where nobody has written down the judgment or the authority that would settle them.

Twelve of the twenty stages have no published role, and nine have no artifact to hand on.

Where this comes from

This draft is written alongside AgentMesh, where the middle of the lifecycle is built and running, and where the gaps at either end were found the ordinary way, by hitting them. Roles are defined at agentroles.ai. What an agent publishes about itself is defined at agentdoc.net. Implementations lists what exists today, stage by stage.

It is a working draft. The stages are stable; the roles and the checks are not, and several are named here before they are written.
